The moment new offers unlock is the moment the app covers them.

Freecash asks before it gives. Rewards and commitment hooks arrive as a stack of blocking surfaces, timed against the user's intent instead of with it.

Evidence · The detail that hurts most

The payoff hides behind adjectives.

The goals read Good, Great, Incredible, Unstoppable. What a goal is worth, “25x more likely to earn $100” for 30 days, shows only after it is tapped.

The sheet asks for a 30-day commitment and keeps the reason off screen. A reward I cannot see cannot motivate the choice.

Why it matters for the business

Revenue follows offers started. This moment taxes the next one.

“Games and apps pay us to find real players.”Freecash onboarding, 00:40 · Observed

The highest-intent moment

I have just earned, and new, higher-value offers have just unlocked. Every surface between “offers unlocked” and “second offer started” taxes it.

The hooks are worth keeping

Streaks and notifications drive return visits. Friend streaks drive acquisition. Stacked and unpriced, they are easy to dismiss. Sequenced and priced, each gets a fair chance.

Trust converts

The brief says it. Pre-ticked consent at 00:13, a permission prompt before value, and hidden rewards all spend it.

Assumption

There is conversion loss between returning from offer one and starting offer two, and between each hook being shown and being accepted. I had no Freecash data, so no number here that I did not observe.

Hypothesis

If the reward and commitment hooks arrive one at a time, inline, with the reward visible before the ask, more users start a second offer in the return session and more users accept each hook.

Because the app stops competing with its own offers.

Proposal A hypothesis to test, not a result.

Decisions

Built from Freecash's own parts. One new signal.

Visual

  • Quests reward tag, raised green pill, prize cards and streak orange. Nothing new to learn.
  • One new signal: a green outline with a soft glow, reserved for the single next action.
  • Yellow stays exclusive to starting an offer. Only Milestone uses it.
  • Goal tiles lead with the multiplier, orange, 18px bold. Days come second.

Interaction

  • Expands in place. The page never dims.
  • The reel decelerates and ticks past a centre marker, so it reads as a draw, not a carousel.
  • Balance counts up with a +0.45€ chip. No second Claim.
  • Tile select rewrites the button: “Commit to 30 days”.
  • Docks to a pill on scroll.

Usability

  • Touch targets 44dp or taller. “Not now” sits in a 44dp row.
  • Reduced motion: the reel cross-fades to the result.
  • Colour is never the only signal: the selected goal has a tick and an outline.
  • Claim state is 168dp, so the first offer stays above the fold on 360 × 780.
Not now

Goal state. 5x and 25x observed; 10x and 40x are placeholders.

Proposal Platform guidance agrees: ask in context and explain the benefit first (Android Developers, “Request runtime permissions”). LinkedIn's profile meter is a precedent for one card pointing at the next step.
Measuring success

Two numbers decide it. The rest keeps it honest.

Primary

Second-offer start rate: share of users who start offer two within 10 minutes of returning from offer one.

Time from Earn tab render to first scroll or offer tap.

Today 44 sTarget under 5 s

Per hook, shown to accepted

Streak goal commits, friend invites sent, home screen adds, notification opt-in (pre-prompt and OS prompt).

Guardrails

Day 1 and day 7 retention, day 2 streak return, streak case claims, offer revenue per new user at day 7, card dismiss rate, first-session uninstalls.

How

A/B on new users with a hold-out. Events on every card state: shown, expanded, accepted, not now, docked tap. Before build, five moderated sessions on the prototype: do people notice the card, understand the queue, find declined asks in Quests?

What would prove it wrong

Hook acceptance drops because inline asks are easier to ignore than modals, and second-offer starts do not rise to make up for it. The test reports both sides.
